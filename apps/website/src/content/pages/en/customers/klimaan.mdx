In the Belgian pilot in Otterbeek, energy community [Klimaan](https://klimaan.be) shows its households when local solar power is plentiful. Households use the **Stroomplanner** (Dutch for "power planner") to plan their electricity use for the greenest hours. It's built on Cofy's [energy directives](/docs/concepts/#module).

## Reading the colours

| Colour | Meaning | Forecast solar output | For a 3–4 kWp installation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span class="swatch" style="background: var(--cofy-directive-2)"></span>Dark green | Well above baseload: best for large use | 40% or more | about 1.5 kW or more |
| <span class="swatch" style="background: var(--cofy-directive-1)"></span>Light green | Above baseload: a relatively good moment | below 40% | up to about 1.5 kW |
| <span class="swatch" style="background: var(--cofy-directive-0)"></span>Grey | Around baseload | below 12% | up to about 0.5 kW |
| <span class="swatch" style="background: var(--cofy-directive--1)"></span>Orange | Lower than baseload | below 3% | up to about 0.1 kW |
| <span class="swatch" style="background: var(--cofy-directive--2)"></span>Red | No solar production | 0% | 0 kW |

Forecast solar output is the share of the solar production in the province of Antwerp that Elia expects to be active.

Light green is set low on purpose, so cloudy and winter days still show their better hours. For large consumption, households aim for dark green. Every home gets the same regional signal: not exact for each roof, but a good indication.

## What to expect through the year

Average hours per day in each colour, calculated by EnergyID on the previous year. In winter, households see mainly grey, with the occasional light-green hour.
